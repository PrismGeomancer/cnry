Fake sites look real. The only way to know is to test them.

Miners used to send a canary down the shaft first. If it went quiet, they turned back. Canary does the same for fake mints, fake airdrops and fake wallet apps, before your real wallet is anywhere near them.

Three tests. Before you connect.

1

Ask around

What do other people say about this site? Canary checks scam reports and community vouches. If nobody has looked at it yet, it tells you so and walks you through a quick check of your own.

2

Spot the fake

Look-alike spelling, brand-new domains, address tricks. If a site is dressed up as something it isn't, Canary notices.

3

Send the canary

Canary shows the site a decoy wallet with a small balance. If the site tries to take it, or asks for something it shouldn't, you hear about it before your real wallet is involved, in plain words.

    How Canary decides

    "Silent" if the site is reported, blocklisted or a close look-alike of a well-known site. "Ruffled" if there are two or more softer flags, like a brand-new domain, scam-style words or no reviews. Otherwise "Singing". Every flag is listed above, with its reason.

    The seed phrase stopper

    See what happens when a recovery phrase lands on a web page.

    Never type your real recovery phrase anywhere on this site. Use the test words. Nothing here is stored or sent.

    What the stopper does

    If you type or paste 12 or 24 recovery words into a web page, Canary stops you before the page can read them. Almost no website ever needs them.

    Decoys and the tripwire

    When the stopper catches a phrase, Canary can scramble up a decoy and hand that to the page instead. It is made up from scratch, not a scrambled copy of yours, so it gives away nothing about your real phrase and opens none of your wallets.

    Canary then keeps watch on the decoy. If a site tries to use it, you get a notification right away.

    What Canary watches for

    • Look-alike addresses
    • Brand-new domains
    • Sites reported by others
    • Requests that let a site move all your tokens
    • Recovery phrases typed into web pages
    • Sites that try to use a decoy phrase you gave them

    What the extension can see

    The address of the site you're on, and when that site asks to connect or sign. It does not read your passwords or your keys.

    The three verdicts

    Canary warns. You decide. It can't see everything, so never sign something you don't understand.
